## The seat most clients cannot fill {#the-seat}

</div>
<div class="ms-rail-body ms-prose">

Every NetSuite implementation has a partner and a client. Almost every NetSuite implementation also has a seat on the client side that nobody really fills, and that gap is where a lot of projects quietly fall apart.

The partner brings a full team: project manager, lead consultant, functional staff, technical staff. They are built to deliver the system the SOW describes. The client side is supposed to mirror this, with a sponsor, a steering committee, a business owner for each module, and a super user learning the system in real time.

Most clients cannot actually do this. The people who would fill those roles already have jobs. The controller is closing the books. The IT lead is keeping the network running. The operations manager is running the warehouse.

The partner does their job. The client does what they can. The seam between the two is where the project breaks, and that seam is the role I fill.

## What I do on your side of the project {#the-role}

The role does not map cleanly to a standard job title. The closest description is a super user, a client-side project manager, and a hands-on resource when the partner needs one, all in one seat.

</div>
<div class="ms-card-grid">
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Super user from kickoff</h3>
    <p>I learn the system as it is being built. By go-live I understand why each configuration decision was made, what the workarounds are, and where the technical debt lives.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Client-side project manager</h3>
    <p>I manage your team's tasks against the partner's plan: UAT prep, data prep, training scheduling, and process documentation. When the partner is waiting on something from your team, I know what it is, who owns it, and when it is coming.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Hands-on when asked</h3>
    <p>CSV imports, saved searches, basic configuration, and data validation, when the partner asks because their team is stretched or your team needs work outside the partner's scope.</p>

## What it looks like week to week {#week-to-week}

I sit in the design sessions to make sure your business teams are following what is being decided, asking real questions, and raising constraints that the partner cannot see from the outside. The partner knows NetSuite. They do not know that your AR team has been working around a quirk in the old system for eight years because nobody told them about it.

A lot of the work is genuinely boring. None of it shows up in a slide deck. All of it has to happen for the project to move, and your internal people do not have time for it.

</div>
<div class="ms-panel">
  <h3>A typical week on an active implementation</h3>
  <ul class="ms-checks">
    <li>Every design session the partner runs</li>
    <li>A fifteen-minute client-side standup: what each business owner finished, what is blocking them, what the partner needs by Friday</li>
    <li>Every partner deliverable reviewed before you sign off, against what the SOW promised and the design sessions decided</li>
    <li>The change order pipeline, so you can tell real scope additions from discovery gaps</li>
    <li>Approvals chased, the issues log maintained, training materials prepared, data extracts validated</li>
  </ul>

## What I will not do {#boundaries}

This is the part of the role that separates a useful client-side resource from one who damages the project.

</div>
<div class="ms-card-grid">
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Change your system without being asked</h3>
    <p>No configuration, no saved searches, no bundles, no workflows on my own initiative. The partner is building the system. That is their job and their professional responsibility.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Go around the partner</h3>
    <p>If I have a concern, I raise it with the partner first. Most of the time there is a reason for the decision that I did not have context on. When the concern is real, the partner and I bring it to the steering committee together.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-tile">
    <h3>Pitch for the next engagement mid-project</h3>
    <p>Who supports the system after go-live is a conversation for you and the partner to have. I do not spend the implementation positioning for it.</p>
  </div>
</div>
<div class="ms-prose ms-outro">

These three lines are what keep a client-side resource from being the kind that sinks implementations.

## If the project is already behind

NetSuite implementations rarely blow up. They drift. The status stays green, the partner stays confident, and the drift is visible months before anyone on the project will say the word problem.

The easy advice is to fire the partner and bring in someone else. I think that usually makes things worse. On one project where the partner was close to a year into the work, my estimate was three to six months for a replacement firm to reach the position the existing one already held, at the client's expense, with the original problems still unresolved.

So the work is harder than finding a better partner. It is helping the client be a better client: clearer on requirements, faster on decisions, more accountable on their own deliverables, so the partner can finish the job they started.

There are [narrow cases where switching is right](/blog/netsuite-switching-implementation-partners), and it is worth checking what transfers before you decide.

</div>

  <h3>Seven signs a project is drifting</h3>

    <li>The change orders keep coming</li>
    <li>Your team goes quiet when the partner says best practice</li>
    <li>The project is 80% done and your team has not touched it</li>
    <li>The build matches what you said, not what you do</li>
    <li>Data migration is still a line item for later</li>
    <li>The date will not move, but everything behind it does</li>

### Client-side resource, multi-division horticulture business

</div>
<div class="work-body">

A horticulture company running retail garden centers, greenhouse growing operations, a landscaping division, and wholesale, with over a thousand employees at seasonal peak, was six months behind on its NetSuite implementation. The partner was delivering. The problem was on the client side. Testing, data migration, and internal project management all depended on people who still had full-time jobs running the business, and the work was not getting done.

I joined as the client-side resource for six months, took over the internal project plan, and drove the workstreams that had stalled: building and running the test cycles, preparing and executing the large data imports, and keeping internal decisions moving so the partner was never waiting on the client. Department leads did the parts only they could do, and I did the rest.

<p class="work-outcome">The company went live on the reset date with no further slip. I rolled off after go-live with the internal team owning the system.</p>

## Why the role is worth hiring for {#why}

</div>
<div class="ms-rail-body ms-prose">

A client-side resource on a typical mid-market implementation costs a fraction of the implementation itself. A delayed go-live costs more. A bad design decision that has to be unwound after go-live costs more. A change order that should have been challenged costs more.

Your partner already has the expertise. What you are buying is attention on your side of the table, from someone with time to read the SOW carefully and ask the awkward questions in design sessions while there is still time for the answers to matter.

Implementation support is scoped to your project, for a defined phase. Scope comes out of a first conversation about where the project stands.

## Frequently asked questions

</div>
<div class="ms-rail-body ms-faq">

### What is NetSuite implementation support?

A senior NetSuite consultant working on the client side of an implementation: SOW and scope review, design validation, change-order control, steering committee, user acceptance testing and knowledge transfer. The partner delivers the system, and I make sure your side of the project holds up its end.

### Do you take over from our implementation partner?

No. The partner delivers the system and that is their job. I work alongside them, I do not make changes in your account without being asked, and I raise concerns with the partner before anyone else.

### When should we bring in client-side support?

Before you sign is best, because the SOW decides most of what follows. Teams also bring me in at kickoff, during testing and data migration, or when a project has slipped. The cheapest time to deal with a problem is before go-live.

### Our implementation is behind schedule. Should we switch partners?

Usually not as a first move. A new partner has to rediscover configuration decisions, integrations and migration choices the current one already knows, and that period is billed to you while the original problems remain. On one project where the partner was close to a year into the work, my estimate was three to six months for a replacement firm to reach the same position. Check what transfers before you decide.

### Will you make changes in our NetSuite account?

Only when asked. The partner is building the system. I take on functional or data work, such as CSV imports, saved searches, basic configuration and data validation, when the partner asks because their team is stretched, or when your team needs work that is outside the scope of the partner.

### Do you also manage the project?

Yes, on the client side. I am PMP certified, and I manage the tasks your team owns against the plan the partner runs: UAT preparation, data preparation, training scheduling and business process documentation. The partner manages their own plan and their own team.

### What happens after go-live?

By go-live I know why each configuration decision was made and where the workarounds are, so that knowledge stays on your side when the partner rolls off. Who supports the system after that is your call: your own team, the partner, or ongoing administration with me. I do not pitch for it during the project.
