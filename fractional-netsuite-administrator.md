## When it fits {#when-it-fits}

The question usually arrives about three months after go-live. The implementation partner has rolled off, the [hypercare period is a memory](/blog/post-go-live-cliff), and the list of things nobody owns is getting longer. A report that finance needs. A role that gives someone too much access. An integration that fails quietly every Tuesday. Eventually a controller or a COO says the sentence out loud: we need somebody who owns NetSuite.

The fit is a company that is live on NetSuite, has real administration needs but not forty hours a week of them, and cares more about the same senior person owning the system than about instant response to every question.

Skip the org chart debate and estimate the actual workload. Look back at the last two or three months and count honestly: support requests, report and search builds, role and permission changes, integration babysitting, month-end support, and the improvement projects that never start.

    <p>Start planning the full-time hire. I can hold the system together while you search and help you interview.</p>

What you give up is just as real. I am one person with other clients, not a bench. You get scheduled presence and clearly stated response expectations, not somebody in a chair from nine to five. If your operation needs a full-time person on site, or coverage around the clock, fractional is the wrong answer, and I will say so early.

If you are still mid-implementation, the gap you have is an owner for the project, not the system. That is [implementation support](/about/#implementation-support), a different engagement.

## Three ways to cover the administrator seat {#compare}

You can hire a full-time administrator, buy a support contract from a partner, or bring in a fractional administrator. I sell one of these three, so read this with that in mind. The figures are what I see in job postings, client budgets, and partner proposals, not a scientific survey.

</div>
<div class="ms-card-grid">
  <div class="ms-compare">
    <h3>Full-time hire</h3>
    <dl>
      <dt>Cost</dt>
      <dd>Experienced administrators in the US mostly earn $95,000 to $140,000 in base salary. With benefits and overhead the loaded cost lands between $120,000 and $190,000 a year, plus recruiting fees and a search that commonly takes two to four months.</dd>
      <dt>Fits</dt>
      <dd>A workload well past one hundred hours a month, or many subsidiaries with heavy customization and constant change.</dd>
      <dt>Watch for</dt>
      <dd>Few people cover the whole range from configuration to SuiteScript and integrations, and the system knowledge leaves with them if they resign.</dd>
    </dl>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-compare">
    <h3>Partner support contract</h3>
    <dl>
      <dt>Cost</dt>
      <dd>Usually a prepaid block of hours, or a monthly retainer against an hourly rate.</dd>
      <dt>Fits</dt>
      <dd>Occasional needs, project bursts, and specialized work where a deep bench earns its keep.</dd>
      <dt>Watch for</dt>
      <dd>It is reactive by design. It answers tickets, and is not set up to review roles or integration errors between them.</dd>
    </dl>
  </div>
  <div class="ms-compare ms-compare--pick">
    <h3>Fractional administrator</h3>
    <dl>
      <dt>Cost</dt>
      <dd>A monthly fee for a defined slice of a senior person. Generally a fraction of the loaded full-time number, with no recruiting fee and no months of searching.</dd>
      <dt>Fits</dt>
      <dd>A live account with real administration needs, but not forty hours a week of them.</dd>
      <dt>Watch for</dt>
      <dd>One person with other clients. You get scheduled presence, not a full-time seat.</dd>
    </dl>
  </div>
</div>
<div class="ms-prose ms-outro">

The reasoning behind each number is in [What a NetSuite Administrator Actually Costs](/blog/netsuite-administrator-cost).

## Frequently asked questions

</div>
<div class="ms-rail-body ms-faq">

### What is a fractional NetSuite administrator?

A senior administrator who covers the role for a defined portion of the month, on an ongoing monthly basis. The same person handles support, configuration, reporting and improvements every month, holding the system knowledge without the cost of a full-time seat.

### How is this different from a partner support contract?

A support contract answers tickets, and for occasional needs that can be enough. It is reactive by design, so nobody is watching roles, integrations or system health between tickets. A fractional administrator is the same person every month, working a prioritized backlog and accountable for the health of the system.

### How do I know how much administration my account needs?

Count it. Look back over the last two or three months at support requests, report and search builds, role and permission changes, integration issues, month-end support and the improvement projects that never started, and put rough hours on each. Many mid-market accounts land between twenty and eighty hours a month.

### How is a fractional NetSuite administrator priced?

By the month rather than by the hour, sized to the workload rather than to a block of time. Scope comes out of a first conversation about your environment, so there is no standard rate to quote before that. The arrangement can grow or shrink as the business changes.

### Do you handle SuiteScript and integrations too?

Yes. Small scripts and integration fixes are part of the regular work, such as a one-click export of purchase order lines to Excel built in SuiteScript. Larger builds are quoted separately as fixed-fee projects, scoped to the work.

### When should we hire a full-time administrator instead?

When the genuine workload runs well past one hundred hours a month, or the environment spans many subsidiaries with heavy customization and constant change. At that scale a dedicated seat is justified. I can keep the system stable during the search and help interview candidates.

### Will we work with the same person every time?

Yes. I scope the work and I do the work, in every session. There are no juniors and no handoffs.
