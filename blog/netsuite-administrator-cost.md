# What a NetSuite Administrator Actually Costs

[[toc]]


## Where This Question Comes From

The question usually arrives about three months after go-live. The implementation partner has rolled off, the [hypercare period is a memory](/blog/post-go-live-cliff), and the list of things nobody owns is getting longer. A report that finance needs. A role that gives someone too much access. An integration that fails quietly every Tuesday. Eventually a controller or a COO says the sentence out loud: we need somebody who owns NetSuite.

Then comes the budget conversation, and it turns out "somebody who owns NetSuite" can mean three very different line items. You can hire a full-time administrator. You can buy a support contract from a partner. Or you can bring in a fractional admin, a senior person who covers the role for a defined slice of their time. The price tags are far enough apart that the choice deserves more thought than it usually gets.

We sell one of these three options, so read this with that in mind. We have also been the in-house administrator, worked inside implementation partners, and cleaned up after all three models when they were the wrong fit. The costs below are what we see in job postings, client budgets, and partner proposals, not a scientific survey.

## Option One: Hire a Full-Time Administrator

The sticker price is the salary. In the US market, postings for experienced NetSuite administrators mostly land between $95,000 and $140,000, with certified people in major metros pushing higher. Benefits, payroll taxes, and overhead add another 25 to 40 percent, so the realistic annual cost of the hire is somewhere between $120,000 and $190,000.

Getting the person costs money too. NetSuite admins are scarce enough that many companies end up using a recruiter, and recruiting fees typically run 20 to 25 percent of first-year salary. The search itself tends to take two to four months, during which the backlog you were hiring for keeps growing.

Those are the visible costs. The quieter ones matter more.

The first is breadth. "NetSuite administrator" describes a spectrum, not a job. Configuration and user support sit at one end. SuiteScript development, integration work, and analytics sit at the other. Very few individual humans cover the whole spectrum well, so a single hire usually means choosing which gaps you will still pay a partner to fill.

The second is utilization. A company running 30 to 60 NetSuite users often does not have forty hours a week of genuine administration work. The role fills up with adjacent duties, or the admin spends real hours inventing projects, or they get bored and start interviewing. Which leads to the third cost.

The third is concentration risk. When one person holds all of the system knowledge and that person resigns, you do not lose an employee, you lose the operating manual. We have seen accounts where the workflows, the scripts, and the reasons behind half the configuration walked out the door with a two-week notice. Rebuilding that knowledge costs far more than any salary line.

None of this means the full-time hire is wrong. At sufficient scale it is clearly right. It means the real cost is the loaded salary plus the recruiting, the remaining skill gaps, and the risk you now need to manage.

## Option Two: A Support Contract with a Partner

Most implementation partners will sell you ongoing support, usually as a prepaid block of hours or a monthly retainer against an hourly rate. Senior consultant rates at established partners generally sit between $150 and $250 an hour, and blocks are often sold in 20 to 40 hour increments.

The appeal is flexibility and a deep bench. Some months you use ten hours, some months forty, and if your question involves an obscure module, somebody at the partner has seen it before. For project bursts and specialized work, this model earns its keep.

The costs show up in how the hours get spent. Tickets route to whoever on the bench is available, which is frequently the newest consultant, billed within sight of the senior rate. Each new face spends the first part of every engagement learning your business on your dime. And the meter runs on investigation as well as resolution, which means the incentive structure quietly rewards the slow path. Twenty hours of block time can evaporate into discovery calls and status notes with the actual fix still ahead of you.

There is also nobody watching the system between tickets. Support contracts are reactive by design. Nobody on the partner side is reviewing your roles, noticing the integration errors, or telling you that a feature you already pay for would remove a spreadsheet from your close.

## Option Three: Fractional Administration

Fractional administration means a senior person covers the administrator role for a defined portion of their week, month after month, for a monthly fee. Same person every time. They learn your business once, hold the context, work a prioritized backlog, and remain accountable for the health of the system rather than the closure of tickets.

On cost, the fair comparison is against the loaded full-time number. A fractional arrangement sized to a typical mid-market workload generally runs a fraction of the $120,000 to $190,000 that a full-time hire really costs, without the recruiting fee, the months of searching, or the severance risk if the workload turns out smaller than expected. You pay for the slice of capacity you use, and the slice can grow or shrink as the business changes.

What you give up is just as real, and worth stating plainly. A fractional admin is one person with other clients, not a bench. You get scheduled presence and clearly stated response expectations, not somebody in a chair from nine to five. If your operation genuinely needs a full-time body on site, or coverage around the clock, fractional is the wrong answer and a good fractional consultant will say so early.

The fit is a company that is live on NetSuite, has real administration needs but not forty hours a week of them, and cares more about the same senior person owning the system than about instant response to every question.

## How to Decide

Skip the org chart debate and estimate the actual workload. Look back at the last two or three months and count honestly: support requests, report and search builds, role and permission changes, integration babysitting, month-end support, and the improvement projects that never start. Put rough hours on it.

If the real number is under ten hours a month, you may only need a support contract or a small fractional arrangement, and the discipline to batch your requests.

If it lands between roughly twenty and eighty hours a month, you are in the range where fractional administration was designed to live. This is also the zone where a full-time hire is hardest to justify and most likely to leave.

If the true number is well past one hundred hours a month, or you run many subsidiaries with heavy customization, start planning the full-time hire. A fractional senior admin can hold the system together while you search and can help you interview, which is a better sequence than hiring in a panic.

And if you are still mid-implementation, this is the wrong question for now. The gap you have is [an owner for the project, not the system](/blog/netsuite-client-side-resource), and that is a different engagement.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a full-time NetSuite administrator cost?

In the US market, experienced NetSuite administrators mostly earn between $95,000 and $140,000 in base salary, and the fully loaded cost with benefits and overhead typically lands between $120,000 and $190,000 per year. Recruiting fees and a search that commonly takes two to four months add to the first-year cost.

### What does fractional NetSuite administration mean?

A senior administrator covers the role for a defined portion of their time on an ongoing monthly basis. The same person handles support, configuration, reporting, and improvements every month, holding the system knowledge without the cost of a full-time seat.

### How many hours of administration does a live NetSuite account need?

It varies with user count, customization, and integrations. Many mid-market accounts generate between twenty and eighty hours of genuine administration work per month. Counting your actual requests, builds, and fixes over the last few months gives a more honest number than any benchmark.

### Is a support contract from a partner enough after go-live?

It can be, if your needs are occasional and you accept a reactive model. Support contracts answer tickets. They do not watch roles, integrations, or system health between tickets, and hours are often consumed by consultants learning your business before fixing the problem.

### When does a full-time NetSuite administrator make sense?

When the genuine workload runs well past one hundred hours a month, or the environment spans many subsidiaries with heavy customization and constant change. At that scale a dedicated seat is justified, and a fractional admin can keep the system stable during the search and help interview candidates.
